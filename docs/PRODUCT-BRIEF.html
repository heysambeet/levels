Product brief · draft for sign-off

Nifty 50 Levels

All fifty NIFTY constituents on one page, each showing where it sits against its 50/100/200-day averages and its 52-week range, with RSI, an alert when a line is crossed — and the news that explains the move.

Written 8 Aug 2026 Data verified against the 7 Aug 2026 session Status awaiting your call on 5 open questions Separate from Market Nudge — own folder, own repo
01

The job it does

In your words: “I need the news just to check why the stock is down 1% or up 1%.”

That sentence is the whole product, and it points at something narrower and more useful than a market app. You are not asking for a feed to browse. You are asking a diagnostic question about a specific stock at a specific moment — something moved, and you want to know where it now stands and what caused it.

So the page is built around one loop: see the move → see the levels it just crossed → see the reason. Everything that does not serve that loop stays out.

This already works — measured, not assumed

On Friday 7 Aug, Bajaj Finance fell 5.84%. Querying the news layer for that stock returns, as the top result: “Why Bajaj Finance and Bajaj Finserv Shares Fell Today: RBI's Draft Proposal Triggers Sell-Off” — India Infoline, timestamped 10:01 that morning, alongside CNBC-TV18's “Explained — Why are Bajaj Finance shares down 6% on Friday.”

That is your question asked and answered end to end, on real data, before a line of product code exists.

03

Can it actually be built — and for free?

Yes. Every source below was hit with real requests on 8 Aug 2026, and independently re-run by a second pass to catch claims that only looked true.

The find that shapes the whole design Measured

NSE retired the endpoint that used to return index constituents, and it now hard-404s. But NSE rebuilt its site, and the replacement route is live, public, and needs no login, no cookie warm-up and no Referer — only a browser User-Agent. One request returns all fifty constituents at once:

1 request · 31.7 KB · 273 ms · 51 rows (the index plus exactly 50 constituents)
Complete for all 50: last price, change, % change, previous close, open, day high/low, volume — and 52-week high and low, plus NSE's own precomputed % distance from each.

This matters more than it sounds. It means the live half of the product is one HTTP call, not fifty — so the page is fast, the free hosting tier is nowhere near its limits, and the current index membership comes from NSE itself rather than a list that silently goes stale.

Two independent sources agree

I computed the 52-week range for Tata Motors PV from raw daily candles and got 294.30 – 739.70. NSE's own figure is 294.3 – 739.7. Different providers, different methods, identical answer — which is the check that tells you the data layer is sound.

One thing today could not test

Today is Saturday and the market is closed, so nothing about live intraday behaviour is measured — not how fast prices update during a session, and not how quickly an explainer headline appears after a move. On weekend data the freshest news ran about 4 hours old.

My honest expectation is a 30–90 minute lag between a move and a published explainer. That is fine for “why did this happen”, and too slow for “why is this happening right now”. One weekday test settles it, and it may change how live the page needs to be.

04

The news problem, stated honestly

This is the part most likely to disappoint if built naively, so it is worth being blunt about.

Asking a news search for a company name returns a lot of things that are not about the company. Measured across all fifty stocks: about 60% of returned items were off-topic, because the search pads thin results with unrelated articles rather than returning fewer. Titan brings back Saturn's moon. Trent brings back a cricket match and a WWII museum. Shriram brings back Madhuri Dixit's husband.

Requiring the company name in the headline removes most of that and leaves 47 of 50 stocks with real coverage. But it does not fix everything — a search for TCS still surfaces a criminal case involving an employee, which passes the filter and tells you nothing about the share price.

The design rule I want to lock

The page shows news near a price move. It never claims the news caused the move. Most 1% moves have no story at all, and an app that always produces an explanation will happily invent one — pairing a random headline with a random move and sounding confident.

So: headlines appear with source and timestamp, ordered by recency, and when nothing relevant exists the page says “no stock-specific news” rather than reaching for filler. That is the same discipline as Market Nudge's no-recommendations rule — report, don't conclude.

One source is exempt, because it is causation rather than correlation: company filings with NSE. When a stock moves and the company filed a board-meeting outcome or press release the same day, that is near-certainly the reason. Roughly half the index files something on a given day, so this complements the headlines rather than replacing them.

05

Traps that would silently break it

Each was hit during validation. Recording them now because every one fails quietly — wrong numbers, not error messages.

Daily candles arrive newest-first Measured

Computing an average without reversing them silently uses last summer's prices. TCS's 50-day average comes out 29% wrong, and its RSI reads 79 instead of 63 — crossing the “overbought” line. The number still looks perfectly plausible, which is what makes it dangerous. Fix: reverse on parse, in one place, with a test.

Fetching all 50 at once gets you banned Measured

Fifty simultaneous requests to the candle API tripped rate limiting and a 573-second block. Sequentially, the same fifty take under 3 seconds. There is no reason to parallelise, and a real cost to trying.

The data API refuses non-browser callers Measured

Default script user-agents get a 403 from Upstox and a dropped connection from NSE — the latter surfaces as a network error rather than an HTTP status, which reads like an outage instead of a rejection. Send a browser user-agent everywhere.

Tata Motors demerged, and one successor is a trap Measured

“TATAMOTORS” no longer exists as a symbol. The index holds TMPV, which has full history and is fine. The other successor, TMCV, has only 183 sessions and cannot have a 200-day average yet. An earlier pass of this research picked the wrong one and nearly recorded a permanent limitation that does not exist. Lesson: take the constituent list from NSE every run, never from memory or a hardcoded file.

Scheduled jobs use non-standard weekday numbers Measured

On the hosting platform, Monday–Friday is 2-6, not the usual 1-5. Writing the obvious thing runs the job on a closed Sunday and skips every Friday. Use named days.

07

Five things only you can decide

Everything above is settled or measured. These are not — and each changes what gets built.

1. How live does “live” need to be?Your call

Prices come in one fast call, so frequent refresh is cheap. But the averages, the 52-week range and RSI only change once a day — and the news that explains a move lags it by perhaps half an hour. Fast refresh mostly buys a flickering price.

ARefresh every 1–2 min while the market is openRecommended
BLive streaming ticks — NSE exposes a public stream, but it adds real complexity
COnce after the close — a daily review page, not a live one
2. Where should alerts arrive?Your call

Browser notifications are unreliable on iPhone — they only work if the page is added to the Home Screen, and fail silently in a normal Safari tab. Telegram is free, works on your lock screen, and needs about three minutes of setup from you (creating a bot, then pressing Start).

ATelegram, plus an always-visible “what fired today” list on the pageRecommended
BOn-page only — nothing to set up, but you must open it to know
CEmail digest after the close
3. What should actually trigger an alert?Your call

This is the one I would most like your input on. Fifty stocks against five levels plus RSI is 300 possible triggers — enough to make alerts worthless within a week. A stock sitting on its 200-day average will cross it repeatedly in one session.

My proposal: alert only on a confirmed cross (price closes the other side of the line, not just touches it), at most once per stock per level per day, and only for a shortlist you choose rather than all fifty.

AConfirmed crossings, on a shortlist you pickRecommended
BAll fifty, but only 200-day crossings and new 52-week highs/lows
CTell me your rules and I'll build exactly those
4. Just for you, or for other people?Your call

A personal tool can assume you know what a 200-day average is. A public one needs to explain itself, carry a disclaimer, and survive people arriving with no context. Market Nudge answered “me” — I don't want to assume this one does too.

APersonal, like Market Nudge
BPublic page anyone can open — levels are arithmetic on public data, so the no-recommendations stance still holds
5. What is it called?Your call

I have deliberately not created the repository yet, because the repo takes the product's name and renaming later is friction. “Nifty 50 Levels” is the folder name and works fine as a working title. Tell me the name and I'll create it.

08

What happens once you answer

Roughly in this order, and each step is checkable on its own:

  • Create the repository under the name you choose, with the brief and validated sources committed first.
  • Build the daily job: pull the fifty constituents, compute the averages and RSI, store them.
  • Build the page: the fifty rows, the levels, the search and sort.
  • Add the news layer for movers only, with the relevance filter.
  • Re-test on a weekday, since intraday behaviour is the one unmeasured thing.
  • Add alerts last, once the rules are settled — they are the easiest part to build and the easiest to get wrong.

Nothing here needs a paid service, a domain, or an API key.
